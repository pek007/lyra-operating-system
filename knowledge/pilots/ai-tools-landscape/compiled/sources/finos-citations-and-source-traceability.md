## Summary
The FINOS guidance argues that AI-generated information should provide clear, verifiable paths back to its source material. It frames citations and source traceability as an integrity mechanism, not just a presentation convenience. The source emphasizes chunk-to-output linkage, metadata preservation, granularity, transparency of sourcing, handling of uncitable content, link rot, and versioning.

## Why it matters
This source is central to the trust model of the pilot. It supports the rule that compiled knowledge should be source-traceable and that eloquent synthesis without provenance is not enough.

## Key ideas
- verifiability requires real source paths
- attribution should be specific and faithful
- metadata preservation is essential
- RAG/chunk linkage matters for integrity
- systems should be honest about when direct citation is not possible
- citation presence is not the same as source quality

## Relevance to this pilot
Helps define the integrity layer and provenance expectations for compiled knowledge artifacts.

## Potential limits / cautions
- governance/control oriented rather than implementation-first
- more prescriptive than experimentally validated
