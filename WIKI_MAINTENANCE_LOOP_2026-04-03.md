# Wiki Maintenance Loop

Date: 2026-04-03
Owner: Lyra
Status: Draft operating loop

## Purpose
Define the recurring operating loop that keeps the wiki useful, current, and compounding rather than allowing it to become stale, noisy, or disconnected from research and planning.

---

## 1. Why this loop exists
A wiki does not stay useful just because pages exist.

Without a maintenance loop, the likely failure modes are:
- stale pages
- missing links
- duplicated concepts
- research that never gets promoted
- plans/actions that stop feeding new knowledge back into the wiki
- a gap between visible wiki structure and current working reality

The wiki maintenance loop exists to prevent those failures.

---

## 2. Core loop

### Step 1 — Review recent raw/evidence outputs
Check what new research, reports, syntheses, or source captures were created since the last pass.

Question:
- what new material may deserve compilation or wiki promotion?

### Step 2 — Review recent operational learning
Check what recent planning/action work revealed.

Questions:
- what knowledge gaps blocked planning or action?
- what concepts/topics were used repeatedly?
- what pages were obviously missing or too thin?

### Step 3 — Review wiki freshness
Check the current wiki itself.

Questions:
- what pages are stale?
- what pages are under-supported?
- what pages should be merged or linked better?
- what hubs/indexes are drifting behind actual content?

### Step 4 — Classify required actions
Each finding should be classified as one of:
- `promote_now`
- `update_existing_page`
- `create_new_page`
- `research_next`
- `watch`
- `not_worth_adding`

### Step 5 — Apply bounded maintenance
Execute only the highest-value bounded maintenance actions in the current pass.

Examples:
- update a hub page
- strengthen a weak page
- promote one synthesis into a wiki page
- create one clearly missing page
- add a missing related-page link

### Step 6 — Record remaining gaps
Anything not handled now should be recorded as a visible gap or next-step item.

---

## 3. Frequency
### Recommended rhythm
- lightweight maintenance: nightly/regular loop where relevant
- deeper structural review: weekly or when a domain changes materially

### Practical rule
The loop should be light enough to run often, but structured enough to keep compounding quality.

---

## 4. What this loop should maintain
- page freshness
- page quality and clarity
- hub/index accuracy
- cross-linking quality
- distinction between raw/evidence and wiki layer
- distinction between wiki and operational truth
- visibility of missing coverage

---

## 5. Promotion rule inside the loop
The loop should not blindly turn everything into wiki content.

### Promote when
- the knowledge is likely reusable
- the page would improve navigation or understanding
- the concept/topic is recurring
- the synthesis is stable enough to become a living page or page update

### Do not promote when
- the artifact is transient
- the material is too weak/noisy
- the page would duplicate existing wiki content
- it belongs in operational truth instead of the wiki

---

## 6. Relationship to nightlies
Nightly cycles should eventually help with:
- identifying promotion candidates
- identifying stale/weak pages
- surfacing knowledge gaps from planning/action
- keeping wiki growth tied to actual work rather than random accumulation

This does not mean every nightly pass should rewrite the wiki heavily.
It means the wiki should be part of the recurring learning/maintenance loop.

---

## 7. Success condition
The wiki maintenance loop is working when:
- the wiki becomes steadily more legible and useful
- useful research reliably finds a path into durable wiki knowledge
- gaps become visible instead of staying vague
- page quality improves over time
- the wiki supports planning/action without becoming the system of record

---

## 8. Bottom line
The wiki should not grow only through ad hoc page creation.
It should be maintained through a recurring loop that:
- reviews new research
- reviews planning/action gaps
- reviews wiki freshness
- applies bounded maintenance
- and records what remains missing
