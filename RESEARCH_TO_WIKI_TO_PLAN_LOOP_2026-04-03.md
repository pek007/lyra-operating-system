# Research -> Wiki -> Plan Loop

Date: 2026-04-03
Owner: Lyra
Status: Draft operating model note

## Purpose
Define the operating loop in which research becomes compiled wiki knowledge, wiki knowledge informs plans and actions, and planning/action generates the next research questions.

---

## 1. Core loop

### Step 1 — Research / evidence gathering
Products, capabilities, domains, or agents gather research inputs such as:
- source documents
- reports
- deep research outputs
- briefs
- comparisons
- external references
- internal notes

### Step 2 — Raw storage
The initial research artifact should be stored in a raw/evidence-like layer, not immediately treated as a wiki page.

Examples:
- a deep research report
- a clipped article
- a PDF conversion
- a captured memo
- a source note

### Step 3 — Compilation
The system then compiles the raw material into reusable knowledge artifacts such as:
- source summaries
- concept pages
- topic pages
- comparison pages
- synthesis notes
- output artifacts worth keeping

### Step 4 — Wiki promotion
Only the durable, reusable, browseable knowledge should be promoted into the wiki layer.

### Step 5 — Planning and action use the wiki
The wiki then informs:
- plans
- priorities
- decisions
- capability work
- product execution
- further research direction

### Step 6 — Planning/action reveals gaps
While using the wiki in planning or action, the system should identify:
- missing knowledge
- weak evidence
- outdated pages
- unanswered questions
- areas needing deeper research

### Step 7 — New research is triggered
Those gaps then generate the next research tasks, and the cycle repeats.

---

## 2. The key rule
All meaningful research should have a path into the wiki.

But not all research artifacts should become wiki pages directly.

### Better rule
- **raw research goes into the raw/evidence layer**
- **compiled reusable knowledge goes into the wiki layer**

This prevents the wiki from becoming a pile of dated reports while still ensuring that research compounds.

---

## 3. Example: deep research report
If you create a deep research report now, the right default is:

### First
Store it as a raw/evidence artifact.

For example:
- a report under a raw/evidence/research path
- not immediately as a wiki page

### Then
Extract and compile what is durable and reusable from it, such as:
- a concept page
- a topic update
- a comparison page
- a synthesis note
- an update to an existing wiki page

### Why
A deep research report is usually:
- source-rich
- time-bound
- often broader or noisier than a wiki page should be

The wiki should capture what remains valuable after compilation.

---

## 4. What belongs where

### Raw / evidence layer
Examples:
- deep research reports
- source captures
- raw articles and notes
- imported documents
- one-off research dumps

### Compiled / wiki layer
Examples:
- concept pages
- topic pages
- comparison pages
- stable synthesis pages
- domain maps
- capability pages

### Operational truth layer
Examples:
- plans
- priorities
- risks
- decisions
- task state
- governance commitments

---

## 5. Why this loop matters
This loop matters because it prevents two common failures:

### Failure 1 — research dies in reports
You do valuable research, but it remains trapped in one-off reports and never compounds.

### Failure 2 — the wiki becomes a noisy archive
Everything gets dumped into the wiki, and the wiki becomes too cluttered to be useful.

The loop solves both by requiring:
- raw preservation
- selective compilation
- wiki promotion only for durable knowledge

---

## 6. Implications for nightly cycles
Nightly cycles should eventually help with all three transitions:

### Research -> raw/evidence
Capture and file new material.

### Raw/evidence -> wiki
Promote durable findings into concepts, topics, syntheses, or updated pages.

### Wiki -> plan/action
Use current wiki knowledge to shape priorities and reveal gaps.

### Plan/action -> new research
Identify the next questions or evidence gaps that need research.

---

## 7. The role of the wiki
The wiki should become the main human-browseable knowledge layer.

It should help answer:
- what do we know?
- what is current?
- what connects to what?
- what is missing or weak?

But it should not become:
- the source-of-record for operational truth
- a raw research archive
- a dump of every generated document

---

## 8. Bottom line
Yes — if you create a deep research report now, the right mental model is:

- store the report first in a raw/evidence-like layer
- then extract what is durable and reusable from it
- promote that into the wiki
- then use the wiki to inform plans and actions
- and let planning/action generate the next research questions

That is the intended operating loop.
