# Strategy

## Strategic objective
Make Security an operable product capability that maintains the current security picture for Lyra OS and `pxs`, detects material changes in exposure and upstream risk, and converts that into explicit posture decisions, capability needs, and prioritized control work.

## Strategic choices
1. Maintain a current security estate picture rather than relying on scattered local awareness.
2. Focus on practical controls, explicit decisions, and reviewable residual-risk framing.
3. Track material surface change across Lyra OS, `pxs`, Google Workspace, integrations, and other newly relevant environments.
4. Monitor OpenClaw and other upstream changes closely enough to make deliberate upgrade, hardening, or deferral decisions.
5. Treat external ecosystem patterns and wrappers as a source of design intelligence, not just product-shopping.
6. Convert evidence, upstream change, and ecosystem findings into capability updates, strategy, roadmap, priorities, and control work.
7. Prevent silent trust-boundary or exposure drift.

## Operating thesis
Security should not behave like a scattered combination of audits, policies, and reactions.
It should function as a living security-intelligence and posture-governance layer for the real operating estate.

## Strategic implications
- Estate awareness is now a first-class Security concern, not just a background assumption.
- New platform introductions such as Google Workspace should be treated as capability-planning triggers.
- Fast-moving OpenClaw releases make upstream monitoring part of operational security, not optional research.
- Auditability and traceability should be treated as explicit capability areas, especially for high-risk actions and external-write surfaces.
