# Delivery Doctrine

Status: Active
Product: Delivery (`A-006`)
Owner: Lyra
Date: 2026-03-15

## Current doctrine

### 1. Delivery credibility depends on proof, not architecture alone
A well-designed delivery model is not enough. Delivery becomes trustworthy only when one real or representative change has moved through the full path with inspectable evidence.

### 2. Gates must become contracts, not interpreted checklists
Delivery quality depends on explicit, risk-aware, evidence-backed gates. Checklist intent is useful, but contract-grade semantics are needed for reproducibility and machine assistance.

### 3. Evidence is part of the product, not just a by-product of execution
Evidence packs, review outputs, and traceability are part of how Delivery proves quality and learns over time.

### 4. Delivery should improve while shipping through itself
The product should use real delivery work to strengthen the delivery system rather than separating delivery architecture from delivery practice.

### 5. Broad radar is required; depth must stay bounded
Delivery should monitor broader developments in AI-native and evidence-backed delivery, but should deepen only the few themes most likely to change our operating model now.

## Known unresolved questions
- What is the smallest convincing first Delivery proof case?
- Which parts of the gate should become machine-checkable first?
- What is the minimum useful weekly scorecard that stays low-noise?
- Which external delivery patterns are genuinely relevant versus just fashionable?

## Confidence and limits
Confidence is high on the need for proof-case execution, contract-grade gating, and operational review loops.
Confidence is moderate on the broader external doctrine because Delivery's research layer has only just been formalized.
